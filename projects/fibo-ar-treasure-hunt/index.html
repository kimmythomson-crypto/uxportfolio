04

FIBO AR Treasure Hunt

Client
RX Global
Year
2023
Role
UX Concept & Design Lead
Focus
AR · B2C

FIBO, one of Europe’s largest fitness shows, was struggling to draw footfall on its consumer days, so exhibitors weren’t buying stands for them. In a six-week design sprint, the team built a web-based AR treasure hunt: visitors scanned branded roll-ups across eight halls to collect 3D treasures and enter a prize draw. It was a fast, honest experiment, and its lessons on onboarding, prizes and scale shaped what came next.

1st

Context

Europe’s largest fitness show

FIBO runs for four days in Cologne: two trade days, then two consumer days over the weekend, with bodybuilding championships, fitness celebrities and HYROX alongside the stands.

Approach

A six-week design sprint

With six weeks from concept to launch, the event team, developers, a product manager and design worked it through in one room, so feasibility was settled as ideas came up.

An AR treasure hunt won. It suited an audience that was mostly under 45, used a new capability in the team, could scale to different prizes and opened the door to exhibitor partnerships.

The game

How to play

  1. 01

    Discover

    Branded roll-ups stand in all eight halls.

  2. 02

    Start

    Scan the roll-up’s QR code to open the web app.

  3. 03

    Register

    Pick a language and scan your FIBO badge.

  4. 04

    Collect

    Scan the sports image to reveal an AR treasure, then collect it.

  5. 05

    Hunt

    Use the hall map to find the rest. Finishers enter a draw for next year’s tickets.

Under the hood

Eight pairs of images

Each treasure paired a scannable trigger image, working like a QR code, with a 3D AR object. Triggers needed clear silhouettes to scan reliably, which took a lot of trial and error.

Most ready-made 3D models were too detailed to run smoothly on phones, so the team settled on lighter, sports-themed models.
